01About

Curious first, technical second.

I’m a Data Science student at USC, living in Los Angeles. The questions I enjoy most are small, everyday ones: when do the bike docks near campus run dry, what actually drives the price of a place to stay, why one coffee line moves twice as fast as the other.

Lately I’ve been spending most of my time on statistics that hold up outside the notebook — time series, Bayesian thinking, and learning to be honest about uncertainty instead of hiding it behind a single number. I also care a lot about the last step: a chart that someone can understand without me standing next to it.

Away from a keyboard, I’m usually hunting for a good pour-over, playing pickup basketball, or wandering around the city with a film camera.

03Projects

Things I’ve built to learn.

Class projects and weekend experiments, mostly with public datasets.

Time series · Forecasting

Forecasting LA bike-share demand

Predicting hourly checkouts at stations around downtown using about two years of public trip data plus weather. The fun part was learning how much a rainy Tuesday breaks every assumption.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • statsmodels
  • scikit-learn

EDA · Regression

What drives Airbnb prices in LA?

An exploratory pass over a public snapshot of roughly 40,000 listings: cleaning, mapping by neighborhood, and a simple regression to see how much location, size, and reviews each explain.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • Seaborn
  • SQL

Machine learning · Recommenders

A movie recommender, from scratch

Matrix factorization on the public MovieLens ratings, built first in NumPy to understand it and then in PyTorch. Mostly a lesson in how to evaluate a model when every user is different.

  • Python
  • NumPy
  • PyTorch

Time series · Side project

How long is the coffee line, really?

A few weeks of wait times I logged by hand at two campus coffee spots, turned into a small time-series study of rush hours. Small data, but it settled a long argument with my roommate.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • Tableau
